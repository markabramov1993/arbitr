## 1. Ten ready-to-post hooks / captions

Each unit is self-contained and includes a direct CTA.

### Hook 1 — The drawer
**Copy:** There is probably a laptop in your drawer that still boots. Before you call it junk, give it one last real job. Old hardware deserves a second chapter.  
**CTA:** Open that drawer tonight and see what still turns on.  
**Format note:** 2-line opening card, then reveal an old laptop boot screen.

### Hook 2 — Upgrade rebellion
**Copy:** The easiest upgrade is not always buying something new. Sometimes it is learning what the machine you already own can still do.  
**CTA:** Pick one forgotten computer and revive one useful task on it this week.  
**Format note:** hard cut from a shopping-cart icon to a screwdriver + terminal.

### Hook 3 — The fan sound
**Copy:** Remember that old startup chime, the click of a hard drive, the fan spinning up? That machine was part of your life. It can still be part of your lab.  
**CTA:** Power it on, document the model, and give it a new purpose.  
**Format note:** sound-led; no brand logos needed.

### Hook 4 — Keep one out of the bin
**Copy:** You do not have to save every old computer. Save one. Learn its story, clean it, boot it, and make it useful again.  
**CTA:** Rescue one machine before your next electronics clear-out.  
**Format note:** simple before/after desk shot.

### Hook 5 — New is not the only interesting
**Copy:** Faster is useful. Newer is convenient. But weird old hardware teaches you things a perfect modern setup hides.  
**CTA:** Build one tiny project on the oldest working machine you can find.  
**Format note:** close-up of ports, keyboard, fan, terminal prompt.

### Hook 6 — The €0 experiment
**Copy:** Before buying another gadget for your next tech experiment, try the computer you already forgot you owned. The most interesting build may start at €0.  
**CTA:** Give yourself 30 minutes with an old machine before buying anything.  
**Format note:** show `€0` as the first-frame hook; this is a cost-of-starting statement, not an earnings claim.

### Hook 7 — Your old PC is a time capsule
**Copy:** Old computers are time capsules you can still interact with. The keyboard wear, stickers, files, ports — all of it tells a story.  
**CTA:** Photograph yours, write down the year, then see what modern task it can still handle.  
**Format note:** warm desk light, slow 3-shot sequence.

### Hook 8 — Refuse disposable tech
**Copy:** Tech culture says replace, replace, replace. You are allowed to be stubborn. Repair it. Reuse it. Learn from it.  
**CTA:** Choose one device you will keep working instead of replacing this month.  
**Format note:** three text punches: `REPAIR / REUSE / LEARN`.

### Hook 9 — Saturday challenge
**Copy:** Weekend challenge: find the oldest computer you own, boot it, connect it safely, and make it do one useful thing. No shopping required.  
**CTA:** Post the model and the one task you brought back to life.  
**Format note:** checklist animation; avoid asking for sensitive screenshots.

### Hook 10 — Start here
**Copy:** Curious about giving old hardware a second life? Start small: one machine, one hour, one useful experiment. That is enough.  
**CTA:** Explore RustChain as one example of a project built around keeping real hardware useful: https://rustchain.org  
**Format note:** quiet final card, URL visible for at least 2 seconds.

## 4. Formatting / production notes

- Hooks are intentionally short enough for X captions and adaptable to TikTok, Reels and Shorts descriptions.

- Visuals should use real contributor-owned hardware photos, simple vector shapes, or newly generated assets with clear rights.
- Avoid trademark-heavy close-ups and do not imply endorsement by a hardware vendor.
- Do not use stock “e-waste mountain” imagery unless the publisher has a valid license.
- No token price, income, investment-return, guaranteed-reward, or environmental-impact number is asserted in this pack.
- `rustchain.org` appears only as an optional discovery CTA, not as a promise of earnings.
